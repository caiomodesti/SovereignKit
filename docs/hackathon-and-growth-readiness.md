# Hackathon, business, roadmap, and expansion readiness

## Executive verdict

SovereignKit is technically credible for a Solana infrastructure/tooling track,
but it is not yet submission-ready. Its strongest differentiator is not a
dashboard: it is a falsifiable evidence pipeline that separates RPC
acknowledgment, ledger observation, broad degradation, and class-selective
behavior without claiming intent.

Current assessment:

| Dimension | Score | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Functionality | 23/30 | controlled pipeline, real failover, and Devnet lifecycle work; no hosted judge-ready demo |
| Innovation | 22/25 | matched probes plus evidence-preserving route intelligence is distinctive |
| Technical execution | 19/20 | strict contracts, tests, fixtures, signatures, and hostile audits |
| Potential impact | 10/15 | credible wallet/protocol/RPC value, but customer pain and adoption are unvalidated |
| Presentation | 7/10 | strong README/report/dashboard; no sub-three-minute demo or public trial path |
| **Total today** | **81/100** | competitive technical project, incomplete submission package |

The project can become a strong hackathon entry after Sprint 12 if judges can
try one clear end-to-end flow without local Agave setup. A private repository,
missing hosted demo, missing short video, and absent named team profile are
submission blockers—not code-quality defects.

## Best hackathon position

- Track: infrastructure or developer tooling.
- Buyer/user shown in the demo: a wallet or protocol reliability engineer.
- Hook: “An RPC accepted your transaction. Did it actually land—and can your
  application prove which fallback restored execution?”
- Core demo: introduce a controlled class-selective route fault, show matched
  evidence classify it as `ASYMMETRIC`, then show the SDK route around it while
  stale intelligence still fails open.
- Proof moment: show the real Devnet transaction separately as compatibility
  evidence, not as the statistical experiment.

Do not pitch the project as a censorship detector, decentralized network, or
universal provider ranking. Those claims are both scientifically unsupported
and commercially counterproductive.

## Business model status

A business model hypothesis exists; a validated business model does not.

The most coherent model is open-core B2B infrastructure:

1. Apache-licensed local SDK, telemetry, controlled probes, and methodology
   create adoption and trust.
2. Managed route intelligence sells fresh multi-region history, alerts, API
   access, and operational convenience.
3. Enterprise assurance sells retention, private deployment, RBAC/SSO, SLA,
   audit exports, and integrations.
4. Private provider diagnostics sells regression monitoring and remediation
   evidence without allowing payment to alter measurements.

Likely economic buyer: reliability/platform engineering first; security or
vendor management for regulated enterprises. Charging providers to avoid a
negative label is explicitly rejected.

Commercial validation gate:

- 15 structured interviews across wallets/apps, execution/protocol/custody,
  and RPC infrastructure;
- at least five recurring pain reports;
- three design partners willing to provide routes or incident data;
- two explicit paid-pilot or budget signals;
- one clearly identified buyer and deployment model.

Until those thresholds are met, pricing and revenue projections would be
fiction.

## Roadmap status

The engineering roadmap is real and nearly complete: Sprints 0–11 are accepted,
with Sprint 12 reserved for security, quality, and demo hardening. What was
missing was a separate post-v0.1 expansion roadmap tied to evidence and customer
gates.

## Expansion roadmap after Sprint 12

### Phase A — Judge-ready and design-partner-ready package

- one-command or hosted read-only demo using retained evidence;
- under-three-minute demo video;
- public repository and reproducible quick start;
- security/secrets/privacy review;
- design-partner interview kit and incident-cost questionnaire.

Gate: an external tester can understand and run the core flow in ten minutes.

### Phase B — Independent-observation pilot

- three separately operated readers across at least two infrastructure and
  regional failure domains;
- durable observer identity rotation and provenance;
- repeated declared windows without naming providers publicly;
- false-positive and missingness measurement on organic conditions.

Gate: operational independence is evidenced, not inferred from logical clients.

### Phase C — Private managed beta

- authenticated hosted snapshot/API;
- tenant isolation, retention, alerting, audit exports, and SLOs;
- private route labels and customer-controlled disclosure;
- two paid or budget-approved pilots.

Gate: a primary buyer repeatedly uses the product during real incidents.

### Phase D — Responsible ecosystem expansion

- additional regions, route types, workload-specific declared classes, and
  client-language integrations;
- calibrated policy versions with published evaluation sets;
- provider right-of-response and responsible-disclosure workflow;
- public aggregate reports only after legal and methodological review.

Gate: public claims remain reproducible and cannot be purchased or suppressed.

### Explicitly deferred

- tokens or observer incentives;
- permissionless observer enrollment;
- public provider league tables;
- automatic censorship or intent attribution;
- Mainnet routing based on uncalibrated public scores.
